## 1. Chosen visual style

**"Grounded sunlight realism."** Photoreal materials and lighting (Unreal Lumen), a
restrained, earthy palette where **glazed tile is precious and rare** — the colour
punctuation of a mostly mud-brick world. Cinematic framing (wide landscapes, long
shadows, dust in light shafts) in the spirit of Ghost of Tsushima, but no stylised
exaggeration and no fantasy.

Principles:
1. **Earth first, tile second.** Most buildings are mud brick, fired brick and plaster;
   turquoise and cobalt tile appear on important monuments, portals and domes only.
2. **Lived-in.** Dust, wear, patched plaster, soot above doorways, textiles everywhere
   (awnings, carpets, felt, saddle cloths).
3. **Readable silhouettes.** Players must read guards, archers and paths at a glance:
   enemies have strong silhouettes (helmet shapes, spears); paths are lit.
4. **Period-true.** In 1387–91, Bibi-Khanym, Gur-e Amir and the Registan madrasas do
   **not** exist yet (see §2.3).
5. **Respect.** Mosques, shrines and tombs are depicted respectfully; no combat inside
   active religious spaces in the main story.

**Building materials (documented):** structural mud brick and fired brick; plaster;
glazed tile; **banna'i** (glazed and unglazed bricks in geometric / square-Kufic
patterns); **mosaic faience** (cut-tile mosaic) for focal areas such as portals; carved
terracotta. Sources: [Iranica: Decoration](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/decoration/),
[Iranica: Architecture v](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/architecture-v/),
[Iranica: Brick](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/brick-blocks-of-tempered-mud/).

### 2.2 Interpretation (our choices)
- **Rabats/caravanserais:** square enclosure, single portal, rooms around a courtyard,
  corner towers; we use mud brick with fired-brick portal and a modest tiled dome on the
  main hall. Exact appearance of a small 1380s road rabat is not documented — this is a
  composite.
- **Village houses:** flat roofs of poplar beams + reed + clay, *aywan* (shaded porches),
  walled courtyards, irrigation ditches (*aryk*) — based on long-standing regional
  vernacular, not on excavated 1380s houses.
- **City quarters (*mahalla*):** narrow lanes, blank walls, carved wooden doors,
  covered market crossings — reconstruction informed by later bazaars.

### 2.3 Anachronism list — do **not** show in 1387–91
Registan madrasas (Ulugh Beg 1417–20; Sher-Dor 1619–36; Tilla-Kari 1646–60 — in 1387
the Registan is a market square) · Bibi-Khanym mosque (1399–1404) · Gur-e Amir (1403) ·
Ulugh Beg observatory (1420s) · Kalyan mosque in its present form (15th–16th c.) ·
modern restored "perfect" surfaces of any monument (UNESCO notes heavy restoration).
Sources: [Archnet Registan](https://www.archnet.org/sites/3069),
[Britannica Ulugh Beg Madrasah](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Ulugh-Beg-Madrasah),
[Archnet Bibi-Khanym](https://www.archnet.org/sites/2464),
[Archnet Gur-e Amir](https://www.archnet.org/sites/2127).

### 3.2 Interpretation (our choices, flagged)
| Item | Game choice | Why it is interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Swords | **Mildly curved sabre** for Turko-Mongol troops; **straight double-edged sword** for some Persian-speaking townsmen/guards | No reputable online source settles 14th-c. blade shapes; avoid the strongly curved Safavid *shamshir* |
| Bow | Short **composite recurve bow**, thumb-ring draw, quiver on the right hip/back | Attested generally (steppe tradition); no period object verified |
| Shields | Round **kalkan** (cane or leather with metal boss) | Attested in later paintings |
| Polearms/maces | Spears, lances for cavalry, flanged maces | Plausible; later paintings |
| Common soldier | Quilted coat, felt or leather cap, some with lamellar | Painted evidence is of elites |
| Bahram | Practical scout: dark quilted kaftan, belted; leather boots; felt cap or wrapped turban; sabre, knife, bow; **no armour beyond a padded coat** (stealth) | Composite |
| Raiders (prototype enemies) | Mixed lamellar/quilted, conical helmets with aventail, earthy reds and browns, felt | Composite |
| **Mirror armour** | **Not used** | Surviving examples are 16th–17th c. ([Met example](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/24007)) |
| Banners/standards | Plain coloured horsetail standards (*tugh*), simple cloth banners | Thin evidence — keep simple, avoid inventing heraldry |
| Horses & tack | Hardy steppe horses (small, stocky) and larger riding horses for elites; wooden saddle frames with high pommel/cantle, short stirrups, felt saddle cloths | General steppe tradition; verify specifics |

**Painting caveat:** most "Timurid look" imagery (fine robes, turbans, colour palettes)
comes from **1430s–1490s** Shiraz and Herat manuscripts — 40–100 years after our period.
Use it for *spirit*, then simplify toward the plainer 14th c.

## 4. Lighting, colour and atmosphere

### 4.1 Palette
| Role | Colour (approx. sRGB) | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Mud brick / earth | `#B89A72`, `#A5835A` | 60–70 % of any city frame |
| Fired brick | `#9C6B48` | Monuments, portals |
| Plaster / lime | `#D9CDB4` | Interiors, wealthy houses |
| Turquoise glaze | `#2E9C9A` | Domes, accents — **sparingly** |
| Cobalt glaze | `#1F3F8F` | Tile accents |
| White glaze | `#E8E4D8` | Banna'i patterns |
| Textile red / madder | `#8E2F25` | Carpets, enemy faction cloth |
| Steppe grass (summer) | `#B4A35E` | Dry, golden |
| Poplar / orchard green | `#5C7A3A` | Oasis contrast |
| Night | desaturated blue `#2A3550` + torch orange `#E0883A` | Infiltration missions |

### 4.2 Lighting guidelines (Unreal)
- **Time of day per mission:** the prototype mission is at **late dusk / blue hour**
  (low sun, long shadows → stealth readability). Market missions at morning; battles at
  dawn.
- Sun: warm (≈ 4500–5500 K at golden hour), strong, hard shadows; Lumen GI bounces warm
  light off brick into shade.
- **Atmosphere:** Sky Atmosphere + Exponential Height Fog with low-lying dust; volumetric
  fog in interiors for light shafts; heat haze only in midday desert.
- **Interiors:** torches and oil lamps (warm, flickering), smoke under domes, dust
  particles.
- **Exposure:** keep shaded areas readable (min EV clamp) — stealth needs players to see
  into shadows while enemies can't.
- **Wind:** constant movement in cloth awnings, grass, poplars; dust gusts.

### 4.3 Sound/visual mood words
Sun-baked · dusty · quiet before violence · echoing courtyards · wind · smoke · warm
brick vs. cold blue tile.

## 5. Notes on historical uncertainty

1. **The 1380s Samarkand skyline is uncertain.** Kok Saray is gone; walls and gate lines
   are partly conjectural (Iranica notes a proposed outer wall without archaeological
   evidence). Our reconstructions will be labelled in the codex.
2. **Restoration bias:** many monuments were heavily restored in the 20th c.; do not
   copy restored surfaces as if original.
3. **Painting time-lag:** visual sources for costume and armour are mostly 40–100 years
   later than our setting.
4. **Primary sources are biased:** Ibn Arabshah (hostile), Yazdi's *Zafarnama*
   (panegyric), Clavijo (outsider, 1404) — see
   [Iranica: Ebn Arabsah](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ebn-arabsah/),
   [Iranica: Historiography v](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/historiography-v/).
5. **Languages:** Persian (administration, literature) and Chagatai Turkic (military
   elite) — [Iranica: Chaghatay language](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/chaghatay-language-and-literature/).
   Signage and inscriptions should be Arabic-script Persian/Arabic, never modern Uzbek.
6. **To research further (open items):** Hermitage, Louvre and David Collection
   catalogues for Timurid metalwork and tiles; B. F. Manz, *The Rise and Rule of
   Tamerlane* (1989) for campaign dates; archaeological reports on Khwarazmian forts.
