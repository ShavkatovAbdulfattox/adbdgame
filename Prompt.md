# MASTER PROMPT: SHADOWS OF TRANSOXIANA
## An Open-World Historical Action Game Set in Medieval Central Asia

Act as a AAA game director, senior gameplay programmer, historical researcher, combat designer, world-building artist, and technical architect.

Your mission is to design and build a playable historical action-adventure game inspired by the atmosphere, stealth, combat, exploration, and cinematic storytelling of Assassin’s Creed, Ghost of Tsushima, and Kingdom Come: Deliverance.

Do NOT copy their characters, maps, missions, assets, or proprietary designs. Create an original game rooted in the history, architecture, cultures, and conflicts of Uzbekistan and Central Asia.

## 1. GAME CONCEPT

Working title: SHADOWS OF TRANSOXIANA

Genre:
- Historical action-adventure
- Third-person melee combat
- Stealth and assassination
- Open-world exploration
- RPG progression
- Historical warfare and political intrigue

Setting:
Medieval Central Asia, primarily the territories of modern Uzbekistan, with locations across Transoxiana, Khwarazm, Khorasan, and surrounding historical regions.

The game should make players feel as though they are walking through the real historical world of Central Asia: enormous armies, ancient cities, desert fortresses, mountain passes, bustling Silk Road markets, royal courts, and dangerous political rivalries.

## 2. HISTORICAL PERIODS AND INSPIRATION

Use historical research to establish a believable world inspired by these periods:

- The Samanid era and the intellectual golden age of Bukhara and Samarkand.
- The Mongol conquests and the destruction and transformation of Central Asian cities in the 13th century.
- The rise of Amir Temur and the Timurid Empire in the 14th century.
- The campaigns, military strategy, diplomacy, and court politics of the Timurid period.
- The later Timurid world of Ulugh Beg, science, astronomy, architecture, and cultural achievements.

IMPORTANT: These periods span centuries and must not be presented as one historically simultaneous setting. Choose a primary period for the main campaign, then introduce earlier or later eras through flashbacks, historical memories, separate campaigns, or explicitly fictional alternate-history content.

Recommended primary setting: 1370–1405, during the rise and reign of Amir Temur.

Use historically grounded geography, clothing, weapons, military organization, architecture, languages, and political relationships. Clearly distinguish documented history from fictional characters and events.

## 3. MAIN CHARACTER

Create an original protagonist born in the region around Samarkand.

The protagonist is a highly trained scout, infiltrator, swordsman, and intelligence operative who becomes involved in the political conflicts surrounding the rise of the Timurid Empire.

Possible backstory:
- His family is caught in a conflict between rival powers.
- He survives an attack on his hometown.
- He learns horsemanship, archery, swordsmanship, survival, and espionage.
- He becomes an agent who must navigate rival commanders, royal families, merchants, spies, and military leaders.
- His ultimate challenge is deciding whether loyalty to the empire is more important than justice for ordinary people.

The protagonist must have a unique personality, believable motivations, weaknesses, relationships, and a character arc.

Do not portray the protagonist as a documented historical figure unless supported by evidence.

## 4. GAME WORLD

Build a richly detailed, explorable world featuring:

SAMARKAND
- Monumental medieval city architecture.
- Royal residences, fortifications, courtyards, markets, workshops, and caravan routes.
- Political intrigue, intelligence missions, and palace infiltration.
- Distinctive blue-tiled architecture where appropriate to the selected date.

BUKHARA
- Religious and scholarly institutions.
- Dense urban streets, markets, caravanserais, and hidden passageways.
- Missions involving merchants, scholars, rival factions, and secret information.

KHWAREZM
- Desert settlements, fortified cities, river landscapes, and caravan routes.
- Survival, siege warfare, and dangerous expeditions.

MOUNTAIN AND STEPPE REGIONS
- Horse-riding exploration.
- Ambushes and scouting missions.
- Nomadic camps, hunting, tracking, and battlefield reconnaissance.

Do not simply create oversized empty terrain. Every region must contain activities, meaningful encounters, environmental storytelling, and historically appropriate settlements.

## 5. CORE GAMEPLAY

### Stealth and infiltration
- Hide in shadows and environmental cover.
- Distract or misdirect guards.
- Disguise yourself where appropriate.
- Scout enemy patrols.
- Infiltrate fortified compounds and military camps.
- Steal documents, recover intelligence, rescue prisoners, and eliminate designated targets.
- Give players multiple approaches to major missions.

### Combat
Create a responsive third-person combat system featuring:
- Swords and sabers appropriate to the setting.
- Daggers and other historically grounded close-combat weapons.
- Bows and mounted archery.
- Dodging, parrying, blocking, and counterattacks.
- Stamina, armor, damage, and enemy attack patterns.
- Different enemy archetypes, including infantry, archers, cavalry, and elite guards.

Combat should feel weighty, tactical, and readable rather than like button-mashing.

Avoid presenting every weapon as historically used in every region or period. Research the selected setting.

### Horseback gameplay
- Ride across open terrain.
- Navigate city streets and rural routes.
- Engage in mounted archery.
- Pursue enemies and escape patrols.
- Travel with caravans.
- Participate in reconnaissance missions before major battles.

### Historical warfare
- Scout enemy formations.
- Sabotage supply lines.
- Deliver intelligence.
- Defend settlements.
- Participate in large battles.
- Command small tactical units in selected missions.
- Experience sieges and battlefield consequences.

Make the player’s contribution meaningful without unrealistically allowing one warrior to defeat entire armies alone.

## 6. STORY AND QUEST DESIGN

Build a main campaign of approximately 15–25 hours, with optional side quests and exploration.

Potential mission concepts:
1. The Ashes of the Homeland — survive an attack and escape.
2. The City of Secrets — enter Samarkand and uncover a political conspiracy.
3. The Silent Caravan — investigate disappearances along a Silk Road route.
4. The Falcon’s Message — deliver intelligence through enemy territory.
5. The Siege Preparations — infiltrate an enemy camp and discover its strategy.
6. The Price of Loyalty — choose between protecting civilians and obeying a commander.
7. The Shadow at Court — expose a conspiracy within the ruling elite.
8. The Battle Before Dawn — perform reconnaissance and sabotage before a major battle.
9. The Broken Alliance — navigate a conflict between rival commanders.
10. The Fate of Transoxiana — make a final decision with political and personal consequences.

These are fictional mission concepts, not claims about documented historical events. Research historical battles and events before attaching real dates or historical figures to them.

Include:
- Main quests.
- Optional character quests.
- Investigation missions.
- Assassination and infiltration contracts.
- Exploration challenges.
- Historical discoveries.
- Choices with consequences.
- Multiple endings where technically and narratively appropriate.

## 7. VISUAL DIRECTION

Aim for a cinematic, realistic historical atmosphere.

Visual references:
- The environmental storytelling and cinematic presentation of Ghost of Tsushima.
- The historical city exploration and stealth atmosphere of Assassin’s Creed.
- The grounded medieval world simulation of Kingdom Come: Deliverance.

Original art direction:
- Sunlit sandstone walls.
- Blue and turquoise glazed tiles.
- Dusty caravan roads.
- Vast steppes and rugged mountains.
- Desert fortresses.
- Historically researched textiles, armor, banners, and weapons.
- Torch-lit interiors, smoke, dust, and dramatic sunlight.
- Dense marketplaces and believable civilian life.

Avoid generic European castles, Vikings, fantasy magic, stereotypical exoticism, and historically inaccurate architecture.

The world should feel unmistakably Central Asian.

## 8. AUDIO AND LANGUAGE

Build an immersive soundscape featuring:
- Wind across the steppe.
- Horse hooves on stone and sand.
- Blacksmiths, merchants, soldiers, and crowds.
- Historically inspired regional musical motifs.
- Traditional instruments used with historical and cultural care.
- Battlefield ambience and realistic weapon sounds.

Support Uzbek and English initially, with Russian as a possible additional language.

Research historical speech and naming conventions. Do not assume modern Uzbek was spoken in exactly its present form during the Timurid period.

## 9. PROGRESSION AND ECONOMY

Implement:
- Skill trees for stealth, combat, archery, horsemanship, and intelligence.
- Equipment upgrades.
- Historically appropriate armor and weapons.
- Reputation with factions.
- Information gathering and contacts.
- Resource management for selected survival and campaign missions.
- Meaningful decisions that affect alliances and access to missions.

Avoid excessive grinding, intrusive monetization, and meaningless collectible systems.

## 10. TECHNICAL IMPLEMENTATION

First assess the available development environment and choose an appropriate engine.

Preferred options:
- Unreal Engine 5 for a high-fidelity third-person 3D experience.
- Unity if the development environment and team skills make it a better fit.
- A browser-based 3D prototype using Three.js if the immediate goal is a quickly playable demo.

Do not attempt to build an entire AAA open-world game in one step.

Start with a polished vertical slice: a small, playable section that demonstrates the intended quality and core gameplay.

The first prototype should include:
1. A third-person player character.
2. A small, detailed Central Asian settlement or fortified outpost.
3. Third-person movement, sprinting, jumping, and camera controls.
4. One functional sword and bow combat loop.
5. A basic stealth system.
6. Enemy patrol AI with detection and alert states.
7. A horse or a clearly defined follow-up milestone for horseback gameplay.
8. One complete mission with an objective, gameplay challenge, and ending.
9. Ambient audio and an immersive environment.
10. A minimal polished HUD, pause menu, and settings menu.

The prototype must actually run. Do not substitute screenshots, static mockups, or nonfunctional buttons for gameplay.

Work like a professional game development team.

PHASE 1 — RESEARCH AND PREPRODUCTION

- Define the protagonist, story, factions, gameplay loop, and art direction.

PHASE 3 — VERTICAL SLICE

- Add a second mission and a small explorable area.

- Test the complete experience.

PHASE 4 — EXPANSION
- Expand the world and quest system.
- Add horseback travel, additional enemies, equipment, and factions.
- Introduce larger battles and deeper story choices.
- Optimize and test each new feature before expansion.
